Charging

Public networks, apps, cables, home installs and solar, as reported by owners in the first weeks of Q ownership.

Public charging & apps

Use PlugShare to plan

Owners' go-to for finding chargers and planning trips (one member is planning Johannesburg → East London in December). Plan around the chargers you find rather than the other way around. All links

The RF card works across networks

The card supplied with the car is linked to the GridCars app. Load funds in that one app, then tap the card. One owner used it successfully on BMW, Mercedes and Rubicon chargers, with no registration needed. Owner report

You don't need ten apps

A newcomer worried about loading money onto every network's app. The consensus in the chat: load one (GridCars) and use the card. Another suggested you can also load only on arrival at a charger. If you've already loaded money on other apps, check each provider's refund/expiry terms. Unconfirmed

Prices vary a lot

A forwarded session receipt at a mall: 35.11 kWh to reach 90% SoC cost "T 289.12" (tokens; roughly 8.2 per kWh if one token ≈ R1). Another owner was unimpressed by a rate at a certain Eskom site (screenshot only, no figures captured). Check tariffs in the app first.

Own cable: when you need one

The most common newcomer question. Owners' rules of thumb:

  • If the unit says SOCKET, bring your own Type 2 AC cable.
  • Combi chargers (AC + DC) usually have a tethered AC cable, said one owner "80% of the time".
  • Older AC units often have no cable at all.
  • BMW and Mercedes AC chargers need your own cable. One mall in Schoemansville was cited as an example.
Ask your dealer whether a Type 2 cable is included with your car, then keep it in the boot.

Battery habits (opinions)

  • One member: don't leave it at 100% or 0% for hours, otherwise "just drive it, the BMS will protect it".
  • Owners say it's an LFP battery and generally safer; some are still wary of parking an EV in a garage attached to the house. This is opinion, not an official guideline. Follow the owner's manual.
  • For the curious: owners have seen 4% as their lowest; one reported driving 6+ km slowly at 0%. Not recommended.

V2L (power things from the car)

The car supports it but ships without an adapter. A third-party adapter reportedly delivers up to about 3.3 kW: a fridge should run easily, camping gear and TV are fine. One owner estimates an ice maker (120–200 W) would use roughly 4% battery over 8 hours. Look for kits with better weather protection if you'll use it outdoors. Owner report
