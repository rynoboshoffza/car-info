Chery Q · Buyer's checklist

What the dealer doesn't tell you

The recurring surprises from owners' first weeks, most important first. Not every dealer behaves this way (see dealer experiences), but enough did that it's worth checking each item before you pay or collect.

Before you collect: get the wall charger, towing hook, logbook/manual and any promised extras (e.g. smash-and-grab film) listed in writing on the handover document, with who supplies them and by when.

  1. The wall charger may not arrive with the car Owner report

    The spec sheet lists a wall charger (and a portable one) as included, and a branch confirmed the wall charger is included, but it ships separately. Owners report it on back order for weeks, being told "next week" repeatedly, or having it couriered about five days after collection. Cars are apparently not shipped with one each.

    Ask: Is the wall charger physically in stock? Get an expected date in writing. Pre-ordering reportedly worked for at least one buyer (arrived on time). Installation is separate and not free; see home charging costs.

  2. Small accessories go missing, check the towing hook Owner report

    One buyer received an accessory pack with one car but not the other, and was told to collect the rest once number plates were issued. Her advice: make sure you get the towing hook at least.

    Ask: Which accessories are standard on my trim? When exactly can I collect the missing ones?

  3. The Q does not come with a V2L adapter Owner report

    The car supports vehicle-to-load (run 220 V devices from the car's charge port, up to roughly 3.3 kW) but no adapter is supplied. Owners buy third-party ones; one is linked on the links page. If you plan to camp or run a fridge/ice maker, budget for it, and look for good weather protection.

  4. You'll need your own AC cable for many public chargers Owner report

    Chargers marked "SOCKET" (and many BMW/Mercedes AC units) have no tethered cable. Owners recommend always carrying your own Type 2 AC cable. Ask the dealer whether one is included and don't assume. See cable guide.

  5. Paperwork and manuals lag behind the sale Owner report

    Several owners had no logbook/manual after collection and unanswered emails for days. One said they had no proof of ownership beyond the bank. Meanwhile a digital manual exists in the car under Apps, but owners call it not very detailed.

    Ask: When will the registration/logbook documents arrive and who do I contact?

  6. "It's ready tomorrow" may not be true Owner report

    One buyer was told the car was sent for smash-and-grab film and could be collected next day. It turned out the film hadn't been done and the car was still dusty; staff didn't know who sold it. Others report windscreen marks that could not be removed, and Q-logo stickers in inconsistent positions across cars.

    Ask: Inspect the car on collection, in daylight. Photograph everything before signing off.

  7. The trim you ordered may not be the trim you get Owner report

    A pre-ordered Comfort was ordered incorrectly by the dealer; the buyer had to take an Elite or wait two months. Another group is waiting on specific colours (beige, purple Elite). Stock moves quickly and by branch, so confirm colour, interior and trim in writing.

  8. Delivery is on you if the car is far away Owner report

    A highly rated small-town branch was praised, but buyers in Johannesburg noted they would have to pay for transport. Some branches will fetch you from home to collect. Factor transport into the real price.

  9. Finance deadlines can be rushed Owner report

    One buyer's finance contact ignored emails for weeks, then on a Saturday asked for a printed, signed document within two hours because it was month-end, otherwise the process would restart. Others found the finance staff friendly, so experience varies by branch.

    Ask: Get documents early. Don't be rushed into signing something you haven't read.

  10. Software gaps the sales pitch skips Owner report

    Owners report a night-time camera failure with no fix yet (dealers just report to Chery), wireless CarPlay that has to be reconnected, and repeated, unanswered questions about Android Auto. See issues & open questions.

    Ask: Is Android Auto supported on my build? Demonstrate CarPlay/AA during your handover rather than assuming. Reportedly, the handover walkthrough (one branch's took about an hour) is where hidden features get explained.

Also reported: there's no spare wheel and no run-flats; there's some tyre-cost anxiety; and one owner said it's worth setting aside money monthly. See tyres & wheels.

Fair play

The same group also had lots of positive stories (fast communication, fetch-and-collect, thorough handovers). Individual salespeople matter more than the brand.
