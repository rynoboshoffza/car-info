Will they wear quickly?

A Facebook post claimed frequent replacement. Owners' view:

  • Why it might: heavy battery, instant torque on driven wheels, and regen adds stress. The Q is described as about 1500–1700 kg (conflicting figures).
  • Why it may not: others report ICE-like wear. It depends heavily on driving style: one BYD owner needed fronts at 15,000 km but drove everything in sport; another suggests 30–35,000 km for a heavier BYD.
  • Powerful FWD cars were described as "chewing tyres".
  • One owner is putting R500 a month into a tyre fund as a precaution.

EV tyres

  • Owners describe EV tyres as having stronger sidewalls and sometimes foam/sponge inserts to reduce noise. The tyres fitted are "EV compatible"; nobody knew exactly what makes them different.
  • Rotate often and fit dedicated EV tyres if you can. Cheaper non-EV tyres are possible but may wear faster. One owner wondered how insurers see this. Unverified
  • Prices seen: tyres from about R1,300 each in one report; flagship brands such as Bridgestone about R2,500. One member predicts imported EV tyres may end up costing more than premium brands.
  • The original Comfort tyre brand is hard to find online; one owner plans to switch to Yokohama (has EV tyres) at replacement.

No spare, no run-flats

The Q has no spare wheel. Some owners wish it had run-flats; others say run-flats ride harshly and need special rims. Plan for a tyre-repair kit and roadside assistance.
