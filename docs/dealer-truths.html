What the dealer doesn't tell you

Owner-reported surprises when buying and collecting. Get the wall charger, accessories, documents and warranty terms in writing on the handover document.

  1. Wall charger: included, bundled, priced in or unavailable depending on the dealer

    The spec sheet lists a wall charger and a portable charger as included, but owners report every outcome: free, paid, priced into the car, or none (details). Some waited weeks on back order or had it couriered about 5 days after collection. Installation is extra (Charging).

    Ask: Is it included? If not, the price? Is it in stock, and when is delivery?

  2. Paint, cosmetic and add-on charges

    • One owner phoned 5 dealers and all tried to charge for paint; one explained "paint" as scratch-and-dent cover. Owners say white is sold as "metallic" and the paint charge applies even when the colour is not.
    • One owner was "forced" to take a cosmetic interior and exterior package. Others report pressure for tyre warranty, cosmetic plans and extended warranty, and one says some dealers hold scarce cars for buyers who pay about R5,000 extra for add-ons.
    • Other dealers charged no paint, add-ons or wall charger (see prices on Overview).
    • Prices for the same car vary a lot. Several owners advise phoning or emailing 5–6 dealers and comparing.

  3. Accessories and starter pack

    • One buyer received an accessory pack with one car but not the other, and was told to collect the rest once plates were issued. Check the towing hook.
    • One car had no tyre sealant bottle in the starter pack, while a second car in the same household did.
    • The RFID charging cards are in a sealed white envelope inside the black service book in the cubby (several owners). Two owners received none from the dealer.
    • The loose physical backup key is supplied (several owners), but one dealer did not know about it. It fits a keyhole under the driver's door handle (lift the handle).

  4. No V2L adapter

    The car supports vehicle-to-load, but no adapter is supplied (Charging).

  5. Own AC cable needed at many public chargers

    See Charging.

    Ask: Is a Type 2 cable included?

  6. Paperwork, manual and plates

    • Several owners had no logbook after collection and emails went unanswered for days.
    • No printed manual reported; the service book has a brochure. The in-car manual (Apps) is not comprehensive.
    • A fuller PDF owner's manual circulates in the owners' group; it is not hosted here. One owner got a manual link from the dealer's website.
    • Number plates and licence disc took 2–3 weeks for several owners; one temporary plate expired before plates arrived. One owner advises asking for a courtesy car.

    Ask: When will registration documents and plates arrive, and who is the contact?

  7. Condition at handover

    • Charge level: one car was handed over at 35%. Ask for 100%.
    • Tyre pressure: several cars were delivered at about 3 bar, above the manual's figure (Tyres).
    • Alignment: one owner's car drifted out of its lane and wore tyres unevenly by 2,100 km; the dealer agreed to pay for alignment (Tyres).
    • Run-in: two owners say nothing was said about the 100 km/h run-in limit (disputed).
    • Collection promises: one buyer was told the car was at smash-and-grab film fitment and ready next day; the film had not been done and staff did not know who sold the car. Others report an unremovable windscreen mark and inconsistent Q-logo sticker positions.

    Ask: Inspect in daylight and photograph the car before signing.

  8. Delivery dates and trim can change

    • Two owners were told stock would arrive within days; after signing the wait became 3–4 weeks or mid-December. One moved to a dealer that gave honest dates.
    • One pre-ordered Comfort was ordered incorrectly by the dealer; the buyer took an Elite or waited two months. One dealer said a Comfort ordered now would arrive in January. A white Comfort pre-order was delivered in beige because that was all there was (the owner now likes it).
    • A dealer said head office asked for its waiting list, likely for allocations.

  9. Transport is the buyer's cost

    Buying from a distant branch means paying for transport, though some dealers delivered free (see prices on Overview). One branch will fetch buyers from home.

  10. Finance deadlines

    One buyer's finance contact was unresponsive for weeks, then asked for a printed, signed document within 2 hours because it was month-end. Other buyers found finance staff helpful. Two owners heard a free wall charger is more likely when you take dealer finance (hearsay), and one says dealers dislike cash buyers.

  11. Warranty and service plan terms are unclear

    Owners report different terms (details).

    Ask: What are the vehicle and battery warranty and service plan terms, in writing, and are they transferable?

  12. Software and feature gaps

    Night camera failure (no fix yet), the Carlinko app not binding to the car, wireless CarPlay reconnecting and no Android Auto: see Issues & questions and Conflicting info. Several owners say dealers know little about Android Auto, V2L or the app; one was told Android Auto has worked since day one.

    Ask: Demonstrate CarPlay and the app at handover, and have the dealer bind the app to the car (check the VIN).

Dealer experiences by province

Dealers and staff are not named. Each report is one owner's account, unverified and quickly out of date.

Western Cape Mixed

Varies by branch. Positive: fast delivery, regular call-backs, thorough small-town branches. Negative: slow replies after payment, late paperwork and accessories, moved collection dates, and poor service at some larger Cape Town branches (one owner advises avoiding the biggest dealers).

Gauteng Mixed

Quick handovers (two cars collected within about 1.5 days), good after-sales replies at some branches, a waiting list at one. Two owners say stock dates changed after signing. One branch described as difficult; one installer used only the top 2 screws mounting a wall box.

KwaZulu-Natal Mostly positive

One-hour handover covering every menu. A dealer offers to fetch buyers from home. One dealer sold an Elite all-inclusive and delivered it to Gauteng.

Other provinces Positive

Limpopo and Free State dealers described as straightforward: two Free State dealers charged neither paint nor a wall charger, and a Limpopo dealer delivered free by truck.

  • The individual salesperson mattered more than the branch.
  • Three or more owners report 2.5–3 weeks of unresolved Carlinko app linking, with dealers saying "we don't know" and no feedback after escalating to Chery SA.
  • Some group members who post stock are sellers; treat those as adverts.
