Charging

Public charging

  • RF card: the card supplied with the car is linked to the GridCars app. Load funds in that app, then tap the card. One owner used it without registration on BMW, Mercedes and Rubicon chargers. A single load plus the card was one owner's advice; another suggested loading only on arrival. Refund and expiry terms of other apps were not discussed.
  • Prices: one forwarded mall session receipt: 35.11 kWh to 90% SoC for "T 289.12" (tokens; about 8.2 per kWh if 1 token = R1). Another owner questioned a rate at an Eskom site (image only).
  • Charger apps and networks: see Providers.

Own cable

  • A unit marked SOCKET needs your own Type 2 AC cable.
  • Combi chargers (AC + DC) usually have a cable attached, "about 80% of the time" per one owner.
  • Older AC units often have no cable.
  • BMW and Mercedes AC chargers need your own cable.

Wall charger current and wiring

Owner discussion, not electrical advice. Have a qualified electrician inspect the installation and issue a compliance certificate.

  • Owners' suggested amps and cable sizes differ: see Conflicting info.
  • One owner's distribution board feels warm while charging; an electrician was booked to check.
  • Other factors raised: cable run length, the size of the feed to any sub-board, and loose connections, which high current exposes.

Solar and inverter

  • One owner charged from 51% to 90% on solar at no cost, with 8–10 kW of panels and about 6.3 kW at the inverter.
  • One owner charges from a 12 kW Deye inverter. On cloudy, rainy days with time-of-use switched off, the car drew about 6.5 kW from the municipal supply while the home battery was full. Their workaround is a separate plug for the portable charger on cloudy days to draw less. Lowering the wall charger current was also suggested.

Plug stuck in the car

  • Owners fixed it by unlocking the car 2–3 times, or by ending charging in the menu and unlocking. At a charger that needs a card, tap the card to release.
  • A member's summary of the manual:
    • Charging finished, car unlocked: plug releases by itself.
    • Charging finished, car locked: unlock the car with the key, then pull the plug.
    • Charging not finished, car locked: press unlock on the key twice in a row.
    • Charging not finished, car unlocked: stop charging on the screen (energy management) or at the charger.
    • Never pull the plug by force. At a DC charger, stop the session first.
  • Emergency release (lock fault): confirm nothing is charging, open the back door, lift the left luggage compartment cover flap and pull the electronic lock cable, then unplug. Do not use it while charging or if orange high-voltage cable is exposed, and not often. If still stuck, call Chery or roadside assistance.

V2L

  • Up to about 3.3 kW via a third-party adapter (not supplied; see Providers). A fridge should run easily.
  • One owner estimates a 120–200 W ice maker would use about 4% battery over 8 hours.
  • One owner asked for a kit with better weather protection; no answer.

Battery

  • One member: avoid long periods at 0% or 100%, otherwise drive it and the BMS protects the battery.
  • Owners have seen 4% as the lowest. One reports driving 6+ km slowly at 0%.
